A reproducible identity, reconstructed from the original

One L. One family.

Use the L as the first letter of Loam. Retain the same L inside the Commons monogram. The outlines and spacing now come from a single editable source.

Design judgment informed by one Claude Desktop consultation in three rounds. Human recognition, legal distinctiveness and long-term durability remain unverified. The canonical LC avatar and Loam social preview were deployed to GitHub on October 18, 2026.

Reproducible lettering

Outlines, not a guessed font.

One set of seven glyphs

l, o, a, m, c, n and s are custom outlined reconstructions. Every repeated o and m references the same path. This is a logo alphabet only, not a general text typeface.

The s is the largest local departure from the original. Its upper terminal and lower curve are deliberately redrawn for a smooth, coherent alphabet; it is not an exact trace.

The glyph grid uses x-height 1000, flat baseline 1000 and l ascender −567. Round forms extend to −25 and 1025. The o uses cubic approximations to a slightly wide ellipse, with a circular counter.

Optical decisions

The 25-unit round overshoot keeps circles from looking short beside flat stems. The original’s wider vertical stem and thinner L foot remain. The m has two equal 379.5-unit counters and translated matching inner arches, with three 233-unit stems. Flat color removes generated texture.

In Loam, the o starts at x=880, with x-height 880 and baseline 1000. Its bounding box overlaps the L’s horizontally, but the contours stay separate. Minimum numerical clearance is approximately 92.2 units—2.9 CSS pixels at the supported wordmark minimum.

Evidence and limits

What is established—and what remains a judgment.

Mechanically checked

  • Original source file hashes are unchanged.
  • Each SVG embeds exactly the same L path from the one master definition.
  • All contours close; cubic extrema fit declared ink bounds and clear-space canvases.
  • SVG references are local; no font or network dependency can change lettering.
  • PNG dimensions, alpha channel and clear borders pass the finite export audit.

Verification receipt · Numerical clearance bounds

Reviewed, not proved

Claude Desktop first read B as “Loam,” then identified a tight L–o join and unequal m counters. After those repairs and a fair comparison at matching x-height, its final review favored B. It withdrew an apparent weight concern after checking the measured source overlay. The review summary records the repairs, changed recommendation and remaining disagreements.

Keep LC for Commons: the owner’s first reading was an L supporting and sheltering a nested C, almost maternally. That makes the intended relationship care for the commons: Loam provides the substrate that lets it flourish. The outward-open form gives this reading room; it does not establish how other viewers will interpret it. Keep L as the product companion, while leaving its standalone distinctiveness unresolved. A future small product icon merits focused design work; the evidence does not justify replacing the whole family.

One model consultation is not a human reader study. Recognition rates, distinctiveness among competing identities, legal clearance and decades of suitability are not established.
