# Identity review, October 18, 2026

This is a bounded design review of the initial master. It is not a human recognition study, a trademark search, or a claim of universal legibility.

## Reference and construction

The earliest transparent Loam Commons image supplies the L, C, and lettering together. It was selected as the reconstruction reference. Later square and social-preview generations drifted in stem width and proportions; the source inventory and measurements retain that evidence.

The original font and exact vector coordinates are unknown. The master regularizes raster irregularities into explicit curves and custom glyph outlines. The s is the largest local departure: its upper terminal and lower curve are deliberate redraws. Every L and repeated glyph is reused from the same source definition.

## Review method

One fresh Claude Desktop conversation, with Opus 5.5 Extra shown in the UI, was used for three rounds. Before exposure, the narrow questions were defined: literal reading of exactly two arrangements, then concrete lettering and spacing defects. The first image supplied no project name, brief, or preferred answer. An explicit misreading or unresolved closure, collision, or clipping defect would block a claim for the corresponding screen minimum. Concrete criticisms were checked against the source before revision.

The two arrangements were a separate L beside “loam,” and the L as the initial followed by “oam.” The first reading was “L loam” for the separate-symbol draft and “Loam” for the integrated draft. A subsequent family review preferred the separate emblem, identified unequal m counters, and flagged a tight L–o join. The final comparison restored the separate emblem’s source proportions and matched lettering x-height across the two arrangements.

## Repairs and decision

- The m counters changed from 401 and 358 units to two 379.5-unit openings with exactly translated inner arches and three 233-unit stems.
- The integrated wordmark’s o moved from x=800 to x=880. Numerical minimum contour clearance increased from about 28.4 to 92.2 units per L height of 1000. The draft was tightly spaced, but the measured contours did not intersect.
- A matched source overlay contradicted a general claim that the reconstructed lettering was heavier. The reviewer withdrew that concern; local redraw differences remain documented.
- The final critique preferred the integrated wordmark: it reads as one word and balances the short name more economically. It favored retaining LC for Commons.

The adopted identity uses integrated L + oam for Loam and LC + loam commons for the institution. The owner’s first impression of LC was that the L supports and shelters the nested C in an almost maternal manner. This intended meaning is consistent with Loam as substrate and Commons as shared stewardship. It is an interpretation, not measured audience understanding.

## Screen observations and unresolved questions

L 24px and LC 48px square canvases, Loam 32px ink height, and Commons 48px ink height were inspected on light and dark backgrounds in Chromium and on a librsvg review plate. No closed counters, missing contours, or clipping were observed in those renderings. Included clear space reduces ink height to 71.4% of each square icon canvas. The LC channel is the limiting small detail: approximately 1.65px at its inspected minimum.

The screenshots and review plates support only those observed cases. Fractional placement, lower contrast, other displays, print and manufacturing are unresolved. Human recognition and distinctiveness were not validated. The standalone L loses the LC’s layering and remains the weakest member for a lasting application icon. That is a focused future design question, not evidence that the complete identity should be replaced.

The numerical clearance calculation uses adaptive Bézier convex-hull bounds and ordinary binary floating arithmetic. It is not a machine-checked real-number proof. Source hashes establish provenance and consistency, not design quality.
